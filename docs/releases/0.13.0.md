# 0.13.0

Release date: 2026-11-21

Every Jellyfin call took a slot from the same pool: a playback transcode, a
trickplay sprite extraction, a single thumbnail and an `ffprobe`. On a client
with one slot, a trickplay job queued behind a transcode, produced no frames,
and Jellyfin killed it after 20 s. Measured on the production client: 23 errors
in 25 attempts and no tile sets. The same box answered `ffmpeg -version`
through the dispatcher in 60 s while one trickplay job held the only slot,
against 4 ms for `local_ffmpeg`.

## What changed

- Jobs are classified from their program and arguments. Light means `ffprobe`,
  `-f image2`, `-frames:v 1`, `-skip_frame nokey`, or MJPEG sprite extraction.
  Everything else, including a remux, is Heavy. ADR 0032.
- Each host has a second pool: `light_concurrency`, default 2. Light and Heavy
  jobs use separate lock files (`light.<n>` next to `slot.<n>`) and separate
  queue lanes, so a Light job never takes a transcode slot and never ranks
  behind a transcode. `concurrency` keeps its meaning and its default.
- A Light job never waits `queue_wait_secs`. It takes a free light slot at
  once. When its light pool is full on a `local = true` host, it runs on the
  client without a slot. Against a remote host only, it gives a light slot 5 s
  before falling back. Measured on a client where a transcode held the only
  heavy slot: a trickplay-shaped job starts in 3 ms at p50 and 5 ms at p99,
  where it used to wait 60 s.
- The local child is killed with `SIGKILL` when its wrapper dies, in its own
  session. `SIGTERM` left ffmpeg writing its trailer, which is the child an
  operator found still running after `kill -9` on the wrapper.
- The janitor clears a slot file whose recorded process is gone, on every host
  including the local one, so `coord/*/slot.*` stops naming a dead pid. The
  kernel already frees the `flock` when its holder dies, so a killed dispatcher
  frees its slot at once: the next job starts in under a second, measured.
- The `started` event records `class` and `pool`, and `rrffmpeg-stats` prints a
  `class` line: Light and Heavy job counts, so the split is visible after the
  fact.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. A config without `light_concurrency` gets 2, and a journal
written before this release still replays: both new fields are defaulted.
